# Ascertainment-bias probe

**What was tested.** The protocol's §14 bias register lists media ascertainment as
the leading threat. This probe measures it directly, using the protocol's own
rules: mechanism terms only, native language, no political qualifiers (§8.3),
aimed at the *control* country's own executives.

**Method.** One neutral native-language query per control country, naming that
country and its corporate leadership. Every returned result classified by whose
death it concerns.

| probe | language | targeted | results | about **Russian**-affiliated people | about a **target-country elite** | other |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P1 | Polish | Poland | 9 | **7** | **0** | 2 |
| P2 | Czech | Czechia | 8 | **4** | 1 | 3 |
| P3 | Hungarian | Hungary | 9 | **4** | **0** | 5 |
| — | | **total** | **26** | **15 (58%)** | **1 (4%)** | 10 |

**Result.** A politically neutral query, written in the control country's own
language and explicitly naming that country's companies and executives, returns
*Russian* cases as the majority of its results and essentially no control-country
elite cases. The single control-country elite case surfaced (a Czech arms-company
director who died in Nuremberg in 1995) falls outside the study window.

**What this does and does not show.**

- It does **not** show that Polish, Czech or Hungarian elites never die this way.
- It shows that **outcome-driven search cannot ascertain control-country cases at
  anything approaching the rate at which it ascertains Russian ones.**

That asymmetry is not noise. It is differential misclassification of the outcome
that acts only on the denominator countries, and it biases the incidence-rate
ratio **upward, in the direction of the study hypothesis**, by an unknown and
unbounded amount. A study that built its case list this way would recover the
original joke no matter what the truth was — which is precisely the failure mode
protocol §27 was written to prevent.

**Why the protocol's countermeasure cannot be applied here.** The prescribed fix
is person-driven ascertainment: enumerate the cohort first, then establish vital
status for each named member individually (§7). That requires retrieving company
filings, national news archives and obituaries for roughly 10,000 named
individuals. Every one of those hosts is blocked in this environment
(`FEASIBILITY.md` §2), and search snippets alone cannot establish that a named
person was *alive* on 2025-12-31 — absence of a search result is not evidence of
survival, and it is systematically more common for less famous people, which is
the fame bias of §14 operating on the denominator.
